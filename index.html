01 / Look back

1964

Birth rates were concentrated in the twenties.

Women aged 25–29 had the highest rate: 198.1 births per 1,000 women. Ages 20–24 were close behind at 190.5.

02 / A different balance

1994

The twenties still led, with a narrower gap.

The rate at ages 25–29 was 126.2, compared with 105.3 at ages 30–34. The age profile was changing.

The gold guide follows the highest-rate age group across the century.

03 / The crossover

2000

The early thirties take the lead.

Ages 30–34 reached 110.2 births per 1,000 women, just above 108.8 at ages 25–29. This was the first year that ages 30–34 led the series.

The dashed marker identifies this change in the leading age group.

04 / The latest picture

2024

The thirties remain central to parenthood.

Ages 30–34 led at 106.0. Rates at ages 20–24 fell from 69.9 in 1994 to 30.6, while rates at ages 35–39 rose from 41.2 to 66.6.

These are population rates for all births. They do not track individual women or identify the age of first parenthood.

Five years · separate profiles · one common rate scale

02 / Forming partnerships

Marriage and partnership tell different stories.

Legal marital status records whether someone has married. Social marital status describes a co-resident partnership. Read them together to see how relationships have changed.

This choice updates all four Census charts in Chapters 2 and 3.

The never-married population extends beyond the twenties.

2021 · Census · selected year · people aged 15+

Among all people aged 30–34, the never-married share rose from 42.1% in 2011 to 49.5% in 2021. Never married is a legal marital status and includes people living in de facto partnerships. Counts also reflect population size and age structure.

Source: ABS Census T04 · Data · Chart specification

Partnership is increasingly separate from registered marriage.

2021 · Census · selected year · current co-resident arrangements

For ages 25–34, registered marriage fell from 40.6% in 2011 to 32.3% in 2021; de facto partnership rose from 19.3% to 24.5%. This table covers usual residents aged 15+ present in households, as defined by Census social marital status.

Source: ABS Census T05 · Data · Chart specification

Marriage and divorce rates have both eased.

Australia · 2005–2025 · rates per 1,000 people aged 16+

Marriage rates moved from 6.9 to 5.3 per 1,000 people aged 16+ between 2005 and 2025; divorce rates moved from 3.3 to 2.2. Divorce processing in 2021 also affects interpretation. Dividing these annual counts does not estimate the probability that a marriage ends in divorce.

Source: ABS Marriages and Divorces 2025 · Data · Chart specification

03 / Living as a family

Family life reaches beyond the couple with young children.

Family types show both change and continuity. Alongside those structures, a larger share of young adults now lives with parents.

This choice updates all four Census charts in Chapters 2 and 3.

Family size differs across parent arrangements.

2021 · Census · selected year · families with dependent children

In 2021, one dependent child accounted for 50.7% of one-parent families in this table, compared with 34.6% of registered-married couple families. Dependent children are under 15, or dependent full-time students aged 15–24. These ribbons describe a snapshot of families, not families moving between categories.

Source: ABS Census T27 · Data · Chart specification

Change sits alongside continuity in family types.

2021 · Census · selected year · six exclusive family categories

  • Couple without resident children
  • Couple with dependent children
  • Couple with non-dependent children only
  • One parent with dependent children
  • One parent with non-dependent children only
  • Other family

Couples without resident children rose from 37.8% of families in 2011 to 38.8% in 2021; couples with dependent children moved from 36.7% to 36.0%. Couples without resident children include both those before parenthood and those whose children have left home. Census confidentiality adjustments can cause small differences between components and totals.

Source: ABS Census family tables · Data · Chart specification

Living with parents has become more common.

HILDA · 2001 and 2024 · weighted estimates

The report flags all three age-group increases as statistically significant: they meet its test for change beyond sampling uncertainty (10% threshold).

Across all people aged 18–29, the share living with parents rose from 39.4% in 2001 to 49.9% in 2024: a reported increase of 10.5 percentage points. Published changes use unrounded values and can differ slightly from subtraction of the displayed endpoints.

Source: HILDA 2026, Table 11.4, p.154 · Data · Chart specification

What the evidence brings together

Family life is changing in its timing, as well as its shape.

Births have shifted towards older ages, completed cohorts have smaller families than those around the series peak, and registered marriage is less common at some younger adult ages. Family-type shares change more gradually, while living with parents has become more common among young adults. The maps show that regional fertility declines are widespread, and that land area and population create different visual impressions.

These sources describe related changes. They do not establish that one change caused another, or trace a single pathway that every Australian family follows.

Definitions and limits
  • Annual fertility rates, completed cohort fertility and the number of resident children measure different things.
  • Census comparisons observe different populations at each date. Family ribbons are snapshots, not individual transitions.
  • All birth-age medians concern all births. Marriage medians, where used in the data, concern all marriages.
  • Dependent children include those under 15 and dependent full-time students aged 15–24. Non-dependent children can still live in the parental home.
  • Regional fertility uses three-year averages. Regional parent ages and birth counts use the years stated on each map.
  • Map boundaries match the 2021 statistical geography. Minor geographic detail is simplified for web display. Population dots are illustrative placements within the correct region; they are not individual addresses. Dot counts are rounded.
  • Blank source values remain unavailable or not applicable; they are not replaced with zero. Confidentiality adjustments and rounding can affect totals.
